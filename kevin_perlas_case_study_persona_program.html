Case Study: Global People Persona Program  ·  ServiceNow Global People  ·  2025
The Product Organization was making decisions based on assumptions about their users. Leadership asked for personas; I delivered a governed research program product teams could use to make prioritization and readiness decisions.
1. CONTEXT
ServiceNow's Global People organization (human resources) and its partners in Digital Technology (DT) were investing heavily in product-led employee experiences, but they were building for employees they didn't deeply understand. Existing sentiment data wasn't focused on the specific employees that products were targeting, leaving teams to base prioritization and design decisions on assumptions. So products didn't always achieve the outcomes desired, either because they weren't adopted as intended or they didn't match their users' needs or mental models.
2. SITUATION
Leadership recognized the gap and they had a vague idea of what a persona was. So with good intentions, they asked for personas. However, there was no standard persona definition, nor was there an understanding of how personas would be used in the product organization to improve end-user outcomes.
3. APPROACH
Clarify the request. When someone up our management chain entrusts us with a directive, we're often compelled to rush to accommodate. In this case, I knew that a seemingly simple request could lead to many wasted days among a significant number of people if we were working towards the wrong objective. So I met with the leader and the product team to fully understand their intentions. First, we disambiguated personas and came to an agreement about what to expect as a deliverable. I then created the workback plan to set expectations about the time, people, collaboration, and investment that would be required.

Build with data and wire it into the work. I collaborated with the SVP of People Operations and Product to define our focus areas, then worked with data science to map how ServiceNow, Workday, and Snowflake each defined the employee segments we served with the products we were prioritizing. From this, we identified 5 personas to focus on and I built a repeatable annual plan for gathering, synthesizing, and delivering persona data to product teams in an actionable format. Each biannual cycle, I ran quantitative and qualitative research on the topics product teams needed most, then synthesized the findings and delivered outputs that addressed their explicit product needs. I then worked with the PMO to embed persona requirements directly into Global People's governance templates (Stakeholder Impact Assessment, Product Initiation Summary, Operational Readiness Checklist), built a self-service discovery guide so COEs without a researcher could still do the work, and ran a roadshow to transfer the capability org-wide.
HRBP persona made data actionable
4. OUTCOMES
The program moved Global People from a one-off persona deliverable to a living research capability. A repeatable 4-step sprint model replaced ad hoc profiling, and embedding persona requirements directly into governance templates made adoption structural rather than optional. Product teams now have a biannually refreshed source of truth on the people they serve.
